# AGENTS.md

## What this is
- Client-only React 19 + Vite 8 + TypeScript SPA called **2D Animation Studio**. It previews 1080×1920 (9:16), 24 fps minimalist stickman animations drawn procedurally on a canvas, and exports them at 24, 30, or 60 fps.
- The app has **two sections behind a left sidebar**: **2D Stickman** (the canvas animation studio) and **Clone Reels** (the ranking-countdown reel builder, merged in from the old Remotion `ranking-reel-generator` project). Deep links: `?section=reels` opens the reel builder (`?reels=1`, `?view=clone-reels` and `?tab=reels` also work). The section is written back into the URL and the app listens for popstate, so a pasted link or the back button switches views live.
  - **The Angry Stickman vs. The Impossible Dart Shot** (`dart-shot`) — default project; a 30-second one-room comedy on the stickman rig: six fixed beats (a smug challenge, a wild miss into the noticeboard, a three-book-and-spoon launcher that fires a dart into his own head, maximum rage, a clock → lampshade → chair pinball shot that lands the bullseye, and a champion celebration where the board drops over his head like a helmet, he bumps the chair and quits). One dartboard, three darts, three books, one ruler, one spoon, one wall clock, one table lamp, one table and one chair; burned-in captions and a fully procedural score.
  - **The Angry Stickman vs. The Impossible Coin Flip** (`coin-flip`) — a 30-second one-room comedy on the stickman rig: six fixed beats (a smug coin flick that misses the glass, the coin returns and bonks his forehead, a three-book-and-spoon launcher that fires the coin into his own shoe, maximum rage, a clock → lampshade → book pinball shot that drops the coin into the glass, and a victory dance where the glass tips over his head and the coin lands on his head). One gold coin, one glass of water, one table, three books, one ruler, one spoon, one wall clock, one table lamp, one table and one chair; burned-in captions and a fully procedural score.
  - **The Ping-Pong Revenge** (`ping-pong-revenge`) — a 30-second one-room comedy on the stickman rig: six fixed beats (arrogant bounce, far-wall ricochet onto his forehead, a three-book ramp that drops the ball on his foot, a rage table-slam, a ceiling → left wall → lamp → cup pinball shot that lands the cup on his head, and a celebration where the ball pops out and the cup slips over his face as a helmet). One orange ball, one white cup, one table, three books and one lamp; burned-in captions and a fully procedural score.
  - **Angry Stickman: Trick-Shot Champion** (`trick-shot-champion`) — a 60-second continuation of the basketball story on the same white court: the hoop falls on his head, four failed trick shots, a rage-training montage, a championship challenge, an impossible backwards shot that rattles in and drops through, and a final gag where the golden trophy swallows his head (only his legs and a thumbs-up stick out).
  - **Max, Rex & Beep — Pop Star** (`pop-star`) — a 58-second vertical pop comedy on a concert stage with three characters, big on-screen captions and an original upbeat pop instrumental (one flat orange accent on a deep violet stage).
  - **The Paper Ball Ricochet — Episode 4** (`paper-ball-part4`) — the hoop falls on his head, he over-prepares, chases a dozen runaway balls, lands one accidental trick shot, celebrates on a podium of basketballs and ends up hanging upside down in the net.
  - **The Paper Ball Ricochet — Part 3** (`paper-ball-part3`) — the Angry Stickman walks a long sidewalk to a minimalist outdoor basketball court, misses a dozen shots, makes one, and eats a final bonk.
  - **The Paper Ball Ricochet — Episode 2** (`paper-ball-sequel`) — the direct sequel episode, one continuous 30s film with 8 beats.
  - **The Paper Ball Ricochet — 30s** (`paper-ball-30`) — the first 30s director's cut, exported as 30s / 60s / 90s.
  - **The Paper Ball Ricochet — 10s** (`paper-ball`) — the original one-take film, exported as 10s / 20s / 30s.
  - **What If Your Shadow Quit?** (`shadow-quit`) — includes optional voice-over and captions.
- There are no bundled image, video, or audio assets. `metadata.json` is descriptive only. No backend, API routes, database, or env vars are used.
- No test suite, ESLint, Prettier, or CI. `npm run lint` is just `tsc --noEmit`.

## Setup and commands
- `npm install` (no lockfile is committed). `npm run dev` serves on port 3000 bound to `0.0.0.0`.
- Verification is `npm run lint`, then `npm run build`.
- `npm run clean` removes `dist` with a cross-platform Node one-liner.
- `DISABLE_HMR=true` is an AI Studio hook that turns off HMR and file watching (see `vite.config.ts`); leave that logic intact.
- Deep links: `?t=4.45` seeds the playhead, `?project=shadow-quit` selects the shadow project, and they combine: `/?project=shadow-quit&t=2`.

## Architecture
- `src/App.tsx` owns the app shell: the sidebar section (`studio` | `reels`), studio state (active tab, `StudioConfig`, playhead, play/mute, export modal). `src/components/*` holds the four tab views, the export modal and `ReelStudio.tsx`.
- `src/types.ts` is the content source of truth: `PROJECTS`, `PAPER_BALL_SCENES`, `SHADOW_QUIT_SCENES`, master prompts, and `DEFAULT_CONFIG`. `SCENES`/`MASTER_PROMPT` are paper-ball aliases for older code.
- `src/animation/paperBallRenderer.ts` is the original 10-second Paper Ball Ricochet world. `src/animation/extendedPaperBallRenderer.ts` is the 30-second director's cut. `src/animation/sequelPaperBallRenderer.ts` is the 30-second Episode 2 sequel (same room, same rig, new 8-beat timeline, plus books / rubber-band launcher). `src/animation/hoopRenderer.ts` is Part 3 (a distant court that grows as he walks, a run of ball-to-face bonks, one slow-motion make, a cut to black). `src/animation/machineRenderer.ts` is Episode 4 (the hoop falls and lies on him, a pile of balls in his arms, a runaway ball chase, a lucky trick shot, a podium of basketballs, and a net that leaves him hanging upside down). `src/animation/trickShotRenderer.ts` is the 60-second trick-shot short. The old hoop snaps on his head and lies broken on the floor for the rest of the film, so his failed shots are against a wall, a trash can and a bucket, and the training montage uses cones/buckets/targets. There is only ever one usable basket: the referee wheels a **portable** hoop (a backboard on a wheeled frame) onto the court at 25.9s while the camera travels with it, then he steps back, gestures at it and walks to the sideline so he is clear of the ball in scene five. It also draws a burned-in narration caption track (orange, black outline, bottom of the frame) that says what happens to the stickman. `src/animation/popStageRenderer.ts` is the 58-second pop short (Max in purple, Rex in blue, Beep the robot, concert lighting, confetti and burned-in captions). `src/animation/canvasRenderer.ts` is the Shadow Quit world. `src/animation/pingPongRenderer.ts` is the 30-second ping-pong short: one white room, one table, three books, one cup, one lamp and one orange ball, with the whole ball path written as a single `ballAt(t)` timeline and a camera clamp that keeps the floor above the caption band. `src/animation/dartShotRenderer.ts` is the 30-second dart short: one white room, a dartboard, three darts, three books, a ruler, a spoon, a clock, a lamp, a table and a chair, with the three dart flights written as one `dartFlight(t)` timeline and the same camera clamp. `src/animation/coinFlipRenderer.ts` is the 30-second coin-flip short: one white room, one gold coin, one clear glass of water, one table, three books, one ruler, one spoon, one wall clock, one table lamp and one chair, with the whole coin path written as one `coinFlight(t)` timeline and the same camera clamp. Each draws one continuous animation; scene labels are metadata, not render dispatchers.
- Episode 2 is a direct sequel: it opens on Episode 1's aftermath (overturned trash can + five scattered paper balls) and reuses the same world coordinates, so prop positions must stay identical across both 30s films. Part 3 keeps the same character rig and adds a walk, a court and one orange ball.
- `src/components/PlayerView.tsx` owns the `requestAnimationFrame` loop, quantizes preview to 24 fps when `config.fpsMode === 24`, and chooses the renderer from `config.activeProject`. Audio cues fire once via a `Set`; `resetAudioCues(target)` must run on every seek/jump or cues will not replay.
- `scripts/` holds the reel pipeline (`analyze-reference`, `generate-visuals`, `generate-voice`). Node 22 runs them directly with type stripping, so they need no extra dependency. See `SETUP-WINDOWS.md`.
- `src/vite-download-plugin.ts` exposes `GET /__reel-download?url=…` from the dev server. It runs the same downloader Node-side (so yt-dlp works), drops the file into `public/source-videos/` and returns the public path, which is what lets the editor pull a YouTube or TikTok link that the browser itself is not allowed to fetch. The endpoint is dev-only and is not part of a production build.
- The same plugin exposes `POST /__reel-upload` with the raw bytes and a URL-encoded `x-file-name` header (a header cannot carry an emoji). It probes the file with `ffprobe`, refuses anything that is not a video, re-encodes a codec the browser cannot decode (HEVC and friends become H.264) and returns the public path. **Choose a file** in the editor goes through it, so a local clip is validated, stored in `public/source-videos/` and previews, trims and exports with the real footage.
  - The browser sniffs the first 512 bytes before uploading and rejects a saved web page outright, so a page saved with an `.mp4` name never reaches the server. The panel says so and points at the two ways out: press **Download video** again, or pick the file from `public/source-videos/`.
  - Picking the same clip twice is fine: the folder already holds the file, and Windows refuses to rename onto an existing one, so the old copy is removed before the new one is written. Without that, every repeat pick came back as a `400` with a raw `EPERM` message.
  - The re-encode is CPU-bound (a 16 s 1080×1920 AV1 clip takes roughly 20 s while the preview is running), so the panel shows a note with the file name and the size for the whole wait instead of looking frozen.
- `GET /__reel-ping` returns the stamp of the running dev server. `App.tsx` polls it and shows a **reload** banner when the stamp changes, so an open tab that still runs the previous bundle says so instead of quietly behaving like the old code. Every failing reel request is logged to the dev-server console with its status, so a console error in the browser can be traced to the request that caused it.
- `GET /__reel-tts?lang=en&text=…` synthesizes the ranking narration on the Node side and returns `audio/mpeg`. It exists because `speechSynthesis` audio never reaches a Web Audio graph and therefore cannot be recorded into an export, and because the speech service has no CORS header. The handler splits a line at ~180 characters (the service answers 400 above ~200), concatenates the MP3 chunks, and keeps recent takes in memory. It is dev-only like the other reel endpoints.
- `index.html` carries a small dev-only script that watches for a dead hot-reload socket (the `vite:ws:disconnect` event and the "failed to connect to websocket" console error) and puts an orange **Reload** bar at the top of the page. A dev server restart changes the HMR token, and the old tab can never reconnect, so it keeps running the old code until it is reloaded. The script does nothing in a production build (the Vite client is not injected there).
- File names are sanitised before they are written: `<>:"/\|?*#%` and control characters become `_`. A `#` in a name breaks the public path (Vite answers with the SPA page instead of the file) and a space or an emoji breaks a plain path, so the returned path is percent encoded as well.
- `scripts/lib/downloadSourceVideo.ts` downloads the source footage and returns the exact local filepath. A direct MP4 link is streamed with fetch (a 2 GiB cap and a `.part` file that is cleaned up on failure); a platform URL (YouTube, TikTok, ...) goes through `yt-dlp` and FFmpeg. The output path is resolved from the folder listing as well as from what yt-dlp prints, because the Windows console mangles non-ASCII titles. Redirects are followed manually and every hop is validated, loopback and private-network hosts are refused unless `allowPrivateHosts` is set, and an `AbortSignal` cancels the download. The same module lives in the Remotion project at `src/downloader/downloadSourceVideo.ts`; the studio copy is the source of truth.
- Audio is 100% procedural in `src/audio/soundEngine.ts` (Web Audio). Preview speech uses the Web Speech API; export voice uses the Web Audio formant synth. Don’t add media files for existing effects.
- `playSynthesizedVoice` is hard-coded to the two Shadow Quit lines. `playSpokenText` is the general formant voice (the vowel group picks F1/F2 and the pitch drifts down across the line); it is **not** used for reel narration any more because the result is not intelligible. `src/viralrank/narration.ts` fetches the real spoken take from `/__reel-tts`, decodes it once into an `AudioBuffer`, and both the preview and the export play that exact clip (`ExportOptions.narrationAudio`). The browser voice in `viralrank/audioEngine.ts` is only a fallback for when the endpoint is unreachable.
- Long scores must stay scheduled in short sections — `playPopTrackSection` (58s pop), `playTrickShotScoreSection` (60s epic pop), `playPingPongScoreSection` (30s comedy score, four 7.5s slices with silence windows for the bonk, the rage and the freeze) and `playDartShotScoreSection` (30s comedy score, four 7.5s slices with silences for the rage wind-up and the final still) all slice their arrangement into chunks. Scheduling a whole track at once creates thousands of live nodes and crashes the tab during a long export.
- Paper Ball cue map: whoosh **2.0s**, trash rim **4.4s**, lamp **6.0s**, fan **7.5s**, right wall **8.6s**, forehead bonk **9.4s**, desk plop **9.6s**. These times are duplicated in `PlayerView.tsx`, `exportVideo.ts`, `AudioLab.tsx`, and `PromptHub.tsx`; keep all four synchronized when re-timing.
- The 30s and Episode 2 cue maps live in `src/audio/cueMaps.ts` (`PAPER_BALL_30_CUES`, `PAPER_BALL_SEQUEL_CUES`) and drive both the preview and the export track from one list. Part 3 uses `PAPER_BALL_PART3_CUES`, the pop short uses `POP_STAR_CUES`, the trick-shot short uses `TRICK_SHOT_CUES`, the ping-pong short uses `PING_PONG_CUES` and the dart short uses `DART_SHOT_CUES` in the same file.
- Export (`src/utils/exportVideo.ts`) is client-side. It renders the requested fps timeline, captures the canvas with explicit frame requests, and mixes the synthesized SFX through a `MediaStreamAudioDestinationNode` into one `MediaRecorder` file. `fixWebmDuration.ts` patches the WebM duration header.
  - **Frame pacing is a fixed capture grid, not a `requestAnimationFrame` loop.** MediaRecorder stamps frames with the wall clock, so a throttled or stalled page freezes the picture while the audio keeps playing. `step()` in `exportVideo.ts` captures at most one frame per `1 / fps` wall-clock slot: rAF drives the foreground on the exact grid, the audio-clock blip pacer keeps a background tab moving, and a 200 ms watchdog covers a stalled audio callback. Keep the capture requests on that grid; no driver may capture twice in one slot.
  - **A suspended AudioContext must never be awaited.** `resume()` stays pending for as long as the browser's autoplay policy blocks it, so awaiting it left the export sitting at 0% with no error at all. The resume is raced against a 1.5 s timeout; if the context still is not running the export continues without an audio track and reports `silent: true` on the result, and the modal says the file has no sound.
  - `ExportOptions` also accepts a custom `renderer`, `duration`, `narration` and `filePrefix`, which is how the Clone Reels workspace exports its own timeline through the same recorder. A caller that has real speech passes `narrationAudio` (decoded takes) or a `prepareNarration` callback on `<ExportModal>`, which is awaited before the recorder starts; two narrators never overlap because a new take fades the previous one out.
  - The modal offers 10s / 20s / 30s lengths for the 10s film and 30s / 60s / 90s for the 30s film, implemented as 1 / 2 / 3 loops of the project timeline. Total duration is `(duration × loopCount) / exportSpeed`; the cue `Set` is cleared at each loop boundary so audio repeats with the visuals. Output frames always advance at the selected fps, including slow-motion exports.
  - The modal also offers **24 / 30 / 60 fps** output (`ExportFps` in `exportVideo.ts`). The rate only changes how often the same timeline is sampled — cue timing and total duration stay identical, and the WebM `DefaultDuration` header is patched to match the chosen rate.
  - **WebM/VP9+Opus is the reliable Chromium path.** Chromium reports MP4 support but cannot mux this WebAudio track into MP4; it produces a one-frame file. `canRecordMp4WithAudio()` restricts MP4 to Safari. Don’t remove that guard without testing an actual exported file.
  - Exports force safe-zone guides off and ignore the preview fps toggle; the export rate comes from the modal.
  - The export modal detects the usable format, shows progress, previews the result, and triggers the download.

- Part 3, Episode 4 and the trick-shot short use the same floor (y=1460), the same hoop (backboard 270x185 at (810, 470), rim (810, 595), pole x=940) and the same character rig. Episode 4 keeps the same hoop (backboard 270x185 at (810, 470), rim (810, 595), pole x=940) and adds a litter of basketballs (radius 62) that accumulates on the floor. The hoop is a moving prop there: it stands, tips onto him, lies on the floor, is knocked upright by a stray ball, and topples for good at the end - the net leaves the hoop at 4.6s and stays tangled on his head until it snags on the fallen support. The court starts far away and scales up as he walks: backboard 270x185 at (810, 470), rim (810, 595), pole x=940, orange ball (radius 70) at (540, 1390). The walk is staged with scrolling sidewalk seams and a growing court scale, and the ball is drawn inside the court transform while the court is distant. In the final beat the hoop tips over toward him (rotating about its base at x=940) and the backboard lands on his head at 28.45s, so the fall is drawn in the hoop transform rather than a separate prop. The film cuts to black at 29.9s.

- The camera is locked; keep prop positions, proportions, and the white/black-only palette consistent across the timeline. The animation itself must have no gradients, textures, or watermark. Five shorts are the exception and burn captions into the picture: the pop short (seven scene captions), the trick-shot short (a narration track that says what happens to the stickman), the ping-pong short (six orange scene captions with a black outline at the bottom of the frame), the dart short (six white captions with a heavy black outline and a red halo) and the coin-flip short (six gold scene captions with a heavy black outline and a gold halo).

## Styling and imports
- Tailwind v4 via `@tailwindcss/vite` and `@import "tailwindcss"` in `src/index.css`; there is intentionally no `tailwind.config.*` or PostCSS config. The studio UI is dark slate/rose.
- `@/*` resolves to the repo root, not `src/` (e.g. `@/src/types`), matching `vite.config.ts` and `tsconfig.json`. Existing code uses relative imports; prefer those.
- `public/` holds the bundled original SVG character art for the Clone Reels (`public/characters/`) plus the generated pipeline output (`public/reels/`, `public/audio/`). Do not add generated video exports to the repo.
- A ranking entry may carry a **video clip** (`RankingVideoClip`): a URL, a manual in/out range, the on-screen length and a manual title. Clips are merged into one countdown in rank order; a title card with the manual title plays before each clip and the rank column runs down the left. Cross-origin clips need `crossOrigin` support on the host or the export cannot capture the frame.
- `src/animation/rankingReelRenderer.ts` is the canvas Clone Reels renderer and it follows the look of the reference ranking reels: a centred two-line headline ("Ranking" in white plus the ranking style in the accent colour, then the title in cyan), the clip drawn full width and letterboxed on a black frame, and a rank column down the left whose numbers run from #1 at the top to the lowest rank at the bottom. A rank is **revealed** once the countdown has reached it: its number turns from grey to the accent colour and its title appears beside it in white, both with a heavy black outline (`outlinedText` strokes first and fills on top). The title card, the intro and the outro use the same headline and outline language.
  - The Clone Reels preview has the same **9:16 safe areas** toggle as the 2D studio (`Safe areas` in the preview header). The guides are the same rose dashed bounds, the right-hand action bar and the bottom caption band; the label sits at the top of the frame because the reels headline owns that band. Like every export, the guides are forced off in the recorded file (`exportVideo.ts` builds `exportConfig` with `showSafeZones: false`).
- The ranking is **dynamic**: the clips are entered in play order, the first is `#N` and the last is `#1`, and `normalizeRankingData` renumbers them, so reordering in the editor renumbers the ranks. The rank, the title and the clip always belong to the same entry.
- `src/reels/categoryStyles.ts` holds the eight ranking-style presets (trick-shots, funny-moments, amazing-skills, top-facts, satisfying, animals, gaming, custom). A preset owns the reel background, the accent, the title colour and the title-card timing; clip ranges and titles are separate, so switching style never resets an edit. The Remotion project keeps a copy at `src/ranking/categoryStyles.ts`.
- `src/reels/blueprint.ts` is the shared model for the Node scripts and the app: the blueprint format, the voice presets, the narration rewriter and the loaders for `public/reels/blueprint.json` and `public/reels/reference.json`.
- The reel itself has a **ranking style** (`RankingVideoData.category`), and the style name is the coloured half of the headline. Every entry has its own category, which the title card shows. The **Merge videos into the ranking** panel in `ReelStudio.tsx` lists every rank with its clip, title, range and length; **Load a sample merge** fills the top three ranks with the bundled clips in `public/samples/` so the flow can be tried without any footage of your own.
- The **Spoken on / Spoken off** switch in the preview header turns the spoken narration off for both the preview and the export (`prepareNarration` resolves to no takes), so a reel can be made without a voice. The mute button only silences the output; the switch removes the narration lines altogether.
  - The switch also turns the **burned-in caption off**: the editor sets `showCaptions: isNarrationOn` on the render config, and the renderer draws the intro, item and outro captions only when that flag is on. The flag travels into the export through the same config, so a silent reel has no caption plate either. The rank title card is not a caption and always stays.
- `src/reels/videoSources.ts` tells page links (YouTube, TikTok, X, Vimeo) apart from media files, because a page link can never be drawn onto the canvas and captured by the export. A rank can also take a **local file** via **Choose a file**: the file is uploaded to the dev server, checked and re-encoded if needed, and the clip lives in `public/source-videos/` from then on, so it previews, trims and exports with the real footage.
- `src/components/ClipEditor.tsx` is the per-rank trimmer: it previews the clip straight from its URL with its own transport, shows the in/out range on a mini timeline, and has **Set start**, **Set end** and **Play in reel** (which jumps the main preview to where that clip plays). It holds its own hidden `<video>` so the source and the merged preview stay independent. It loads with `crossOrigin="anonymous"` first so the frame can be drawn; if the host refuses, it retries without it and reports that the clip can only be previewed, not captured.
